# Threat model

Inheirit holds money (vaults) and secrets (sealed notes) on behalf of someone who, by design, may not be around to defend them. This document lists who might attack it, what they want, and what stops them, including where the defenses are weaker than we'd like.

## Assets

- **Vault funds:** NEAR held by the contract `inheirit.testnet`; Sepolia ETH and BTC testnet4 at addresses derived by NEAR Chain Signatures from the contract account and `heirloom/<owner>/<chain>`.
- **Sealed notes:** instructions encrypted to the agent's X25519 key and stored on-chain as ciphertext.
- **Liveness data:** when the owner was last active on each source (timestamps only).

## Attackers and defenses

| Attacker | Goal | Defenses | Residual risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Greedy heir**, possibly with a colluding guardian | Get funds or secrets while the owner is alive | Execution needs a quorum of guardians who **accepted** the role (threshold set by the owner; guardians can't be heirs, the owner or duplicates). An unarmed will (too few acceptances) never leaves Active. Then a challenge period during which the owner is notified on every channel. Any strong or medium sign of life (signed NEAR or Sepolia tx, signed heartbeat, Telegram reply from the linked chat) or a single guardian saying "alive" cancels. Release is staged 10/20/30/40 with the **longest wait after the first slice**, and a sign of life mid-execution halts the rest. **Secrets come last:** notes are re-sealed only in `SecretsReleased`, after distribution plus a buffer. | A quorum of colluding guardians plus an owner who is truly unreachable for the whole challenge period (e.g. in hospital with no phone). Then the first 10% moves, and the owner has the long first wait (90 days by default) to notice before anything else does. |
| **Lawyer**, possibly with a colluding heir | Speed up release with a false death certificate | `attest_death_proof` only switches the remaining waits to the accelerated schedule. It is accepted only in GuardianVote, Challenge or Executing. During GuardianVote it does **not** replace the guardian quorum. It never skips the challenge period or a stage, and the remaining percentages are unchanged. A sign of life still cancels, and clears the acceleration. | The accelerated waits (14 days by default) give the owner less time to notice after the first slice. |
| **Curious observer** | Learn who inherits | The app, the agent API and notifications don't reveal heirs before execution (`heirs_hidden`, on by default): the heir page suggests no heir accounts, and heirs are only notified from Executing on. | **UI-level only:** heir accounts and NEAR shares are in contract state, which anyone can query. Commit-reveal of heirs is on the roadmap. |
| **Outsider** | Steal vault funds | Only the contract can request signatures for the vault paths, because the path is derived under the contract account and built by the contract itself. The contract forwards a signature request only from a registered agent with approved TDX measurements, only while that will is Executing (or for an owner-requested withdrawal), and at most 16 signatures per (stage, chain). Owner withdrawals must be signed by the owner. | Bugs in the contract or the template's attestation verification (unaudited). |
| **Compromised or buggy agent** | Sign arbitrary transactions from a vault | Registration requires a TDX attestation matching approved measurements. | **The gate is payload-blind:** the contract can't see what a 32-byte payload hash signs. While a will is Executing, a compromised agent could get up to 16 signatures per (stage, chain) for any transaction from that vault. The defense is the attested code itself plus the cap. |
| **Platform / developer (us)** | Read sealed notes or wills | Notes are encrypted in the owner's browser to a key derived inside the TEE and kept only in memory; plain-English wills go only to attested NEAR AI models. The agent's code is pinned by compose hash, and every new hash must be approved on the contract before an agent can register; all hashes are published in `deploy/tee-agent.json` and shown in the attestation badges under *Signs of life*. | **Centralized KMS:** with Phala's KMS, the Phala account holder can push a new compose in place, and that code would derive the **same** sealed-notes key; the contract's measurement approval gates registration, not key release. So "the developer can't read notes" rests on operational discipline plus Phala, not on cryptography alone. Planned fix: dstack's on-chain KMS with a timelocked compose allowlist. |
| **Contract operator key** (us) | Redeploy the contract or drain every NEAR vault | The contract account `inheirit.testnet` has one full-access key, held by the operator's machine, and `paid-outside.testnet` is the contract owner (`update_contract`, `approve_measurements`). Since the 2026-11-06 audit this key is **never sent into the TEE**: the agent's sponsor is a separate account with no key on the contract, and `tee-agent.ts` refuses to push a sponsor key that is an access key on the contract. | Whoever holds the operator key can deploy any code to the contract account. That code can drain the NEAR vaults and request MPC signatures for every vault path, bypassing all state gating. This is the same operator trust as the centralized KMS row. Production fix: a timelocked multisig as the contract owner, and no full-access key on the contract account. |
| **Stranger with a vault address** | Block the final release (and so the secrets) by sending the BTC vault many tiny UTXOs, exhausting the 16 signatures per (stage, chain) | A stage tx spends at most 8 UTXOs, the largest, and skips UTXOs worth less than their own spend fee or already spent. Signatures are saved as they arrive, so a timeout or restart never asks again for one already obtained. | UTXOs beyond the 8 largest stay in the vault after the last stage (a griefer's gift; a real owner should keep a vault to a few UTXOs). |
| **Account hijacker after death** | Keep the will alive forever (block heirs) by posting from the owner's accounts | Weak social signals alone never count; it takes a strong signal (signed transaction or heartbeat) or a reply from the Telegram chat the owner linked through an owner-signed, one-time link. | Someone holding the owner's NEAR keys, or the owner's phone with Telegram, can keep the will alive. A guardian supermajority override with evidence is on the roadmap. |
| **Telegram impersonator** | Bind their own chat to someone else's will, to keep it alive | A chat is linked only through a one-time, 15-minute deep-link token issued on an NEP-413 request signed with the account's full-access key. Tokens are single-use, and a reply from an unlinked chat is ignored. Replies are stored as timestamps only. | Anyone who gets a fresh link URL before the owner opens it could bind their chat. The link is shown only to the signing browser. |
| **Payout redirector** | Make an heir's inheritance go to their own address | An heir's payout choice (asset, chain, address) is accepted only from a request signed by that heir's NEAR account (NEP-413, checked against on-chain keys), only for an account that is an heir of the will, and only if the address matches the chain's format and a dry 1Click quote succeeds. Each batch has one deposit address and its tx hash is saved first, so a slice is never paid twice; refunds go back to the vault. | Whoever holds the heir's NEAR key can change the heir's choice. On testnet the onward swap is SIMULATED; on mainnet, 1Click's solvers and bridges are trusted for delivery (refund on failure). |
| **Mainnet demo operator mistakes** | Spend more real money than intended | Only `scripts/mainnet-payout.ts` touches mainnet: caps of $5 per transaction and $25 in total (ledger in `~/.config/inheirit`), a fresh quote printed in full before every send, and a typed "yes". The agent itself holds no mainnet keys. | The caps and the ledger are local to the human's machine. |
| **Griefer on the demo agent** | Keep someone else's demo will from ever executing by posting SIMULATED signals for it, or by pointing its watched EVM address at a busy wallet | `/demo/signal` and `/api/owners` must be signed by the will's owner (NEP-413): a SIMULATED Gmail or Calendar signal weighs 0.7 and counts alone, and a busy EVM address would look like a strong signal on every poll. (Both were unsigned in demo mode until the 2026-11-06 break-it and audit sessions.) Every agent write route is now signed in every mode. | Demo mode only; real deployments have no simulated sources. |
| **Spammer** | Drain the operator's NEAR AI credit, gas or demo faucet | AI routes and the demo faucet require NEP-413 signatures checked against on-chain keys, plus per-account and global hourly/daily limits. The faucet funds each will once, only Active wills with empty vaults, in demo mode only. | Free testnet accounts make Sybil accounts cheap; the global limits bound the total cost. |
| **Onboarding farmer** | Drain the testnet account that funds sign-ups, so judges can't sign in | Google, demo, top-up and cast accounts are funded only through `/api/onboard`: per-IP limits, one top-up per account per day, an hourly NEAR budget per server instance, a 3 NEAR floor on the parent, and the Vercel Firewall rate limit on the route. The parent holds onboarding funds only and has no key on the will contract | Limits are per serverless instance and in memory; a determined farmer with many IPs can still empty it. It's testnet NEAR, and `scripts/new-onboard-parent.ts` refills it |
| **Leaked Google ID token** | Create someone's deterministic account with the attacker's key, locking them out | The browser asks Google to sign `sha256(publicKey)` into the token as its nonce, and the server refuses a token whose nonce doesn't match the submitted key | None known |
| **Heartbeat flooder** | Turn many signed heartbeats into RPC load that stalls every will | Signs of life are rate-limited per account; immediate ticks for one will are merged and spaced at least 5 s apart; a key not on its account is remembered for 30 s so made-up keys don't each cost an RPC call | A flood from many funded accounts still costs RPC; the hosted agent uses a keyed RPC |
| **Start-over abuser** | Recover storage the contract paid for (execution records, released notes) by replacing a Completed will, again and again | Replacing a Completed will charges the owner for growth but never refunds below the old storage use; released notes are kept for heirs | None known |
| **Note copier** | Read someone's sealed note before they die, by copying its public ciphertext into a throwaway will and rushing that will to *Secrets released* | Each note is sealed together with the owner and heir it was written for; the agent opens it inside the TEE and re-seals it only when both match the will and note it found it in, and never releases an unbound note | None known (notes sealed before 2026-11-12 were unbound and are never released) |
| **Gas griefer** | Stall every will by making the agent pay for transactions until its account is empty | In Active, signs of life are recorded on-chain only when they move the quiet clock meaningfully; Telegram replies and messages are capped; a watchdog tops the agent up from the TEE sponsor a few times a day | A determined attacker can still cost the sponsor a few NEAR a day |
| **Owner's own mistakes** | Forgetting, losing keys | No check-ins. The owner can cancel at any stage and withdraw while Active (NEAR by the contract; ETH and BTC sent by the agent, signed for that withdrawal only). The will's own vault or an heir's address is refused as the owner's watched EVM address, so the release can't count as a sign of life. Heirs download a backup of their note key. | A lost heir key after notes are released cannot be recovered (the key is fixed once a note is released to it). |

## Operational risks

- **Contract account loss = vault loss.** Vault addresses derive from `inheirit.testnet`. If that account were deleted and re-registered by someone else, they would control every vault. Never delete it. (We learned this on a spike account that stranded 0.06 Sepolia ETH.)
- **Agent app loss = sealed-notes key loss.** The key is bound to one Phala app_id. The agent is upgraded in place and never re-created; if the app is lost, owners must re-seal their notes to a new key (rotation needs the contract owner).
- **MPC latency.** A signature takes up to ~1 minute; BTC needs one per input. The executor saves signed transactions before broadcasting and relies on nonces/UTXOs so a retry can't double-send.
- **Demo keys in the browser.** The demo creates testnet accounts whose secret keys are stored in the browser's localStorage. Fine for throwaway testnet accounts; wallet users keep their keys in the wallet.

## Out of scope

Mainnet funds, legal validity of the will, and attacks on NEAR, Phala, Intel TDX, NVIDIA attestation or the MPC network themselves.
